## 3. Stones: what you need to be able to say

### Stone 4, D Speaking & Stones: Talking about how you feel

You should be able to ask someone about their health and explain how you feel.

- *How are you doing?*
- *Are you feeling any better?*
- *What's the matter with her?*
- *I've had the flu since yesterday.*

### Stone 5, D Speaking & Stones: Talking about what happened

You should be able to describe an accident and say what happened.

- *What were you doing when the accident happened?*
- *I was running when I sprained my ankle.*
- *He lost his balance because he wasn't paying attention.*

### Stone 6, H Speaking & Stones: Giving advice and reassurance

You should be able to give advice, talk about seeing a doctor and reassure someone.

- *You look unwell.*
- *Shouldn't you see a doctor?*
- *Perhaps you could take a break?*
- *Hang in there. I'm sure it'll be fine.*

**Practise:** Hide the English in the Stones tables on **p. 128** and try to say or write the phrases from the Dutch prompts. Also practise putting these expressions into your own short dialogue or email.

## 4. Grammar 3, E Writing & Grammar: Present perfect

**Form: `have / has + past participle` (voltooid deelwoord)**

| Sentence type | Example |
|---|---|
| Positive | *She has visited the doctor.* |
| Negative | *I haven't finished my homework.* |
| Question | *Have you ever broken a bone?* |

The book uses the present perfect for **experiences** and for situations that **started in the past and are still going on**, as well as actions that matter now.

- *I have lived here all my life.*
- *She has had a headache since Monday.*

You need to know:

- [ ] **have** with *I/you/we/they* and **has** with *he/she/it*.
- [ ] How to form positive sentences, negatives and questions.
- [ ] The **past participle**, especially with irregular verbs: *go–went–gone*, *break–broke–broken*, *see–saw–seen*.
- [ ] How to use clues such as *ever, for* and *since*.
- [ ] How to use the **verb in brackets** when the instruction says to fill in the present perfect.

**Read the instruction carefully:** *Complete using the present perfect* means you must use **have/has + past participle**, not simply the past simple.

## 5. Grammar 4, I Writing & Grammar: Can, could, should and have to

| Form | What it expresses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **can** | Ability or permission | *I can walk again.* |
| **could** | Past ability, possibility or a polite request | *I could swim when I was seven.* |
| **should** | Advice | *You should rest.* |
| **have to** | Obligation or necessity | *I have to take my medicine.* |

The **example test** also gives **must** and **(to) be able to** as possible answers. Learn to recognise them in context as well.

- [ ] I can choose the modal that fits the **meaning of the complete sentence**.
- [ ] I recognise clues for past ability, permission, advice and obligation.
- [ ] I understand negative forms such as *can't, couldn't, shouldn't, mustn't* and *don't have to*.
- [ ] I notice whether the instructions say that a word **can be used more than once**.

**Remember:** *mustn't* means something is not allowed; *don't have to* means something is not necessary.

## 6. Irregular verbs: learn both directions

**Stepping Stones Grammar Survey 14, pp. 218–220** lists irregular verbs with their **infinitive, past simple, past participle and Dutch meaning**. These forms are especially useful for **Grammar 3 (present perfect)**.

Practise in **both directions**:

| Task direction | What you need to produce | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Dutch to English** | Give the English verb and its three forms. | *breken* = **break – broke – broken** |
| **English to Dutch** | Recognise an English form and give its Dutch meaning. | **brought** comes from *bring* = **brengen** |
| **In a grammar sentence** | Choose the form demanded by the instruction. | *She has ___ (break) her arm.* = **broken** |

**Difference to remember:** *She broke her arm yesterday* uses the **past simple**; *She has broken her arm* uses the **past participle** after *has*.


## 7. Understand the question before answering: TASK

Some mistakes happen because students know the English but answer a **different task** from the one requested. Before every exercise, use this routine:

| Step | Question to ask yourself | What to mark |
|---|---|---|
| **T: Task** | What must I do? | Circle *match, complete, choose, write* or *translate*. |
| **A: Answer** | What kind of answer is needed? | A word, a definition, a verb form or a whole email? |
| **S: Special conditions** | What limits or extra rules are there? | Underline *present perfect, extra words, more than once, four points, 100–120 words*. |
| **K: Keep checking** | Did I answer exactly what was asked? | Reread the question and check the answer format. |
