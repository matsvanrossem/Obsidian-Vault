I began my teaching placement in September 2026 with a Bachelor's degree in English Language but almost no experience of planning or teaching lessons independently. From an early stage, I was largely responsible for my own classes. I had expected more guidance, but instructions often arrived in brief emails less than 48 hours before a lesson, with limited explanation of how to put them into practice. Trying to understand the curriculum, school procedures and classroom expectations at the same time was exhausting.
This experience also made me resourceful. I sought advice from experienced teachers, observed their approaches and asked questions about how the school operated. I relied heavily on detailed lesson plans and visual structure, partly because preparation gave me confidence when other aspects of teaching felt uncertain. Over time, this developed into a strong sense of ownership over my lessons.
My first fully independent grammar lesson with 2 VWO on 17 September made the distinction between lesson preparation and classroom management clear. I chose the Past Simple because its structure seemed manageable. Although I delivered most of the instruction successfully, I struggled to maintain order during independent work. Earlier partial lessons had already suggested that this would be a challenge.
By 28 October, I felt more confident. In a 2 VWO test recap lesson, I used a diagnostic LessonUp quiz to identify who needed additional explanation and who could complete a more challenging task. Students responded well to this differentiation, and I was pleased that I could manage two learning routes.
I then experimented with a 30 Seconds vocabulary game in 3 HAVO. Students struggled to describe words in English and to follow written rules. Similar difficulties had appeared in the Chapter 1 test, so I introduced the TASK strategy in both 2 VWO and 3 HAVO: Task word, Answer form, Special conditions and Keep checking. This gave students a practical method for interpreting questions instead of simply being told to read more carefully.
I now see my strengths in preparation, subject knowledge, creativity and relationships with students. My werkplekbegeleider has also recognised my enthusiasm, visual support and willingness to learn. Classroom management, evaluating learning more systematically and gaining experience beyond my own lessons remain areas for development.

## 2. Reflection

Most of my learning has come through teaching, experimenting and examining lessons that did not go as planned. One important discovery is that my preference for avoiding confrontation can prevent me from correcting disruptive behaviour promptly. This has been especially apparent in 3 HAVO. On 5 November, I deliberately became more direct about expectations and corrections. Students seemed to respond, although I need to see whether this approach works consistently.
I value positive relationships with students and prefer to speak with them after disciplinary measures. I want them to understand that I am correcting behaviour, not attacking them personally. I am learning that warmth and authority are not opposites: clear boundaries can help create a respectful environment.
I generally find 2 VWO easier to teach because I experience more willingness to engage with the subject than in 3 HAVO. Recognising this preference is important. I should not expect all groups to respond alike, or let my enjoyment of one class determine the effort I invest in another.
My lesson designs have also helped me connect theory with practice. The diagnostic quiz used formative assessment to guide differentiation. TASK draws on explicit instruction and scaffolding to help students develop independence. Meanwhile, the 30 Seconds lesson showed me that an active task is not automatically an effective one: students also need modelling and checks that they understand the instructions.
I sometimes wanted more specific feedback than I received, but I have learned to seek information from colleagues and identify patterns myself. I am moving from asking whether a lesson went well to asking why it went as it did and what I can change next time.
Over the coming weeks, I will correct disruptions earlier, state expectations clearly and follow through calmly, especially in 3 HAVO. After lessons, I will record situations in which I intervened effectively or hesitated. I will also ask my supervisor to observe a lesson specifically for classroom management and give me concrete feedback. This will help me combine authority with the supportive relationships I value.

I will continue teaching TASK, model how to interpret assignments and ask students to explain instructions before starting work. I will use short diagnostic questions or exit tickets to check understanding, then review whether students can apply the strategy independently. I also want to compare experiences with colleagues and use their suggestions to refine my approach. This should help me adapt lessons based on evidence rather than assumptions.

I love English language and literature, but I do not see English as an end in itself. My students have different interests and ambitions, and I want the subject to help them develop transferable skills such as communication, critical thinking and understanding other perspectives.

I also want to recognise the individual behind each student, offering interest, advice and goodwill where appropriate. My aim is not to make everyone love English literature, but to contribute positively to the people they are becoming.
