I found this observation somewhat meagre, as it mainly consisted of questions rather than concrete feedback. Nevertheless, looking back, I can recognise some of the underlying issues.

I have since realised that my tendency to avoid confrontation has been one of the main obstacles in my classroom management, particularly in 3 HAVO. By trying to avoid conflict, I sometimes hesitate to correct students when necessary, which can allow disruptive behaviour to continue.

The observation also asked me to consider the differences between my classes. I generally prefer teaching 2 VWO over 3 HAVO, mainly because I experience a stronger work ethic and greater willingness to engage with the material in 2 VWO. However, I realise that being able to motivate and manage classes with different attitudes towards learning is an important part of becoming a teacher.

On **5 November 2026**, I consciously decided to change my approach in 3 HAVO by being more direct and assertive when addressing disruptive behaviour. I noticed that students seemed to respond positively to this change. Although it is too early to draw conclusions from a single lesson, it showed me that being more consistent and willing to confront unwanted behaviour can make a difference.

Whilst I would have appreciated more detailed feedback from my Internship supervisor, the observation did raise questions that have become increasingly relevant to my development. I feel that my classroom management has improved considerably, and I am beginning to understand that establishing authority does not necessarily mean being confrontational, but rather being clear, consistent and willing to correct students when needed, even when this leads to punitive measures. 

One thing I find particularly important in my approach to classroom management is talking to students after giving them a punishment or other disciplinary measure. I want students to understand that my corrections are not personal attacks, but responses to specific behaviour that I consider unacceptable. 

I believe it is important to separate the student from their behaviour and make it clear that I do not think less of them as a person because of something they have done. By having a conversation afterwards, I hope to maintain a positive relationship with the student while still reinforcing my expectations and boundaries. I also want students to feel that they are being treated fairly and that a disciplinary measure does not affect how I approach them in future lessons. For me, this is an important part of finding a balance between being approachable and establishing authority in the classroom.
