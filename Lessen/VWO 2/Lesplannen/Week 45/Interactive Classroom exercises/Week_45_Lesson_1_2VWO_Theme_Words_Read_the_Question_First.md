## Why this lesson is different

The Chapter 1 test suggested a **task-reading problem** as well as possible gaps in grammar or vocabulary knowledge. Some students may know a word or rule but still lose marks because they miss **what the question tells them to do**. This lesson teaches **vocabulary and question-decoding together**. It is **not** a general reading-comprehension lesson and does **not** introduce a new grammar topic.

Students practise a repeatable routine with short, test-style **vocabulary instructions**, then apply the same routine to their actual Stepping Stones exercises. One brief example makes the link to grammar questions from the previous chapter.

## Teach one reusable strategy: TASK

Display on the board and leave it visible for the whole lesson.

| Step | Student question | What to mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T: Task word** | What action must I perform? | **Circle** *translate, complete, choose, match, correct, write, underline*. |
| **A: Answer form** | What must the answer look like? | **Box** *in English / in Dutch*, *a word / a sentence*, or *A, B, C*. |
| **S: Special conditions** | Is there a trap or extra instruction? | **Underline** *two*, *NOT*, *past simple*, *only*, *each*, *one word*. |
| **K: Keep checking** | Does my answer match the question, not just the topic? | Reread the instruction and check **language, number, form, and spelling**. |

**Student mantra:** **Read it. Mark it. Say what to do. Only then answer it.**

**Model on the board (think aloud):**

> **Translate** *wachtkamer* **into English**. Write **one word or phrase**.

- **T:** I have to **translate**; I am not choosing an option.
- **A:** The direction is **Dutch to English**. The answer is not a full sentence.
- **S:** Just **one word or phrase**.
- **K:** My answer is **waiting room**. I check that I did not write Dutch again.

**Contrast:** *Choose the correct English translation of huisarts* requires **selecting an option**; *Write the English translation of huisarts* requires **producing GP**. Students must not assume every vocabulary exercise asks for the same response.

| ------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

Ask: “Why would one lose a mark even if they recognise the word?” Do not frame it as a general reading text.                                                                                 | Think, vote A/B, explain the **direction** mistake to a partner.                                                                                                                          |
| **4-7 min**   | **Activate prior knowledge: Spot the instruction** | Show three short instructions: *Choose the correct word*; *Write TWO words*; *Write the word that does NOT fit*. Ask students to say what a successful answer would look like **without answering**.                                                                                                          | Mini-whiteboard response: *choose / write two / select the exception*. Teacher notes how many immediately miss *TWO* or *NOT*.                                                            |
| **7-13 min**  | **Explicit teaching and modelling: TASK**          | Introduce the four TASK steps. Think aloud with *wachtkamer*, then lead a second model using *GP = huisarts*. Rapidly introduce/retrieve 6-8 B vocabulary items so students have words to work with.                                                                                                          | Circle/box/underline on the sheet. Tell a partner the language and answer form **before** providing the vocabulary answer.                                                                |
| **13-20 min** | **Guided practice: Instruction sort**              | Give pairs student cards **1-4**. Ask them to put them in groups: *translate*, *choose*, *complete*, *correct*. Prompt: “Which exact word told you?” Check all four types together.                                                                                                                           | Pair-sort, mark T/A/S, and explain the expected answer format. **No solving until both partners agree on the task.**                                                                      |
| **20-30 min** | **Collaborative practice: Decode, Do, Check**      | Groups of three use student cards **5-10**. Roles: **Decoder** marks the question; **Solver** answers; **Checker** rereads instructions and verifies the response. Rotate roles every two cards. Offer the vocabulary list openly for the first round: this isolates instruction-reading from word knowledge. | Each group marks, explains, answers, and checks. Students say: **“The task is ___; I need to write ___ in ___.”** Teacher listens for confusion about *NOT*, number, or answer direction. |
| **30-38 min** | **Transfer: Real Stepping Stones exercises 10-13** | Direct students to **B Theme Words, exercises 10-13**. Before tackling each instruction, require T/A/S markings and a quick partner explanation. Do not presume the wording of these exercises; use the instructions actually printed in the book/digital platform.                                           | Independently decode the instruction, then complete as many assigned exercise items as time allows. Partners check the **instruction fit first**, then the word.                          |
| **38-42 min** | **Wrong-answer clinic + grammar bridge**           | Display two flawed responses from the teacher key. First: *Write the English word for huisarts*: response *huisarts*. Second: *Complete with the **past simple** of go: Yesterday I ___ to the GP*: response *was going*. Ask: “Is the issue reading the task, knowing the word/rule, or both?”               | Think-pair-share. Correct the responses: *GP* and *went*. Explain that the **same TASK routine** works in vocabulary **and grammar** questions. No grammar reteaching.                    |
| **42-45 min** | **Exit ticket: Decode before answering**           | Give the exit ticket from the student sheet. Collect **both** the markings and the answers. Categorise errors: **I = instruction**, **V = vocabulary**, **B = both**, and (if applicable) **G = grammar form**.                                                                                               | Independently mark one prompt and answer it. Name one TASK step they will use in the next test.                                                                                           |

**Pacing:** Prioritise the instruction routine and the transfer to genuine book tasks over finishing every exercise in class. Unfinished exercises 10-13 can be completed as homework. Give short answers at pace; spend talk time on **how students knew what to do**.

## Teacher talk moves

Use these questions repeatedly, especially before allowing students to answer:

- “Which **word in the instruction** tells you what action to take?”
- “Are you **choosing**, **writing**, **translating** or **correcting**?”
- “English to Dutch, or Dutch to English? How do you know?”
- “How many answers? Any words like **not**, **only** or **past simple**?”
- “Could your vocabulary be correct but your **response format** still be wrong?”
- “Read your final answer next to the **instruction**, not just the sentence.”

## Differentiation

**Students who struggle to decode instructions**

- Give the four TASK prompts on a small desk card. Use three marking conventions: circle action, box answer format/direction, underline special conditions.
- Start with **two** cards and a visible word bank. Ask them to **say the task in Dutch** if that helps, then answer in the language required by the question.
- Ask them to complete the sentence **“I must [action] [how many] [in which language/form].”**
- Assess whether the student can decode an instruction **even if the vocabulary answer is wrong**.

**Students who know the task but lack vocabulary**

- Let them use the supplied B vocabulary sheet initially; then remove the support for one retrieval check.
- Choose a small focus set: *appointment, cast, dentist, GP, prescription, waiting room, X-ray*.

**Students ready for challenge**

- Write a misleading test-style instruction using *NOT*, *TWO*, or a changed translation direction; swap with a classmate.
- Explain why a plausible answer would still lose a mark **because it does not follow the instruction**.
- Apply TASK independently to an example from an earlier grammar exercise and identify the tense requirement without solving a new grammar unit.

## Formative assessment: two things to measure

Do not group all wrong answers together as “doesn't know English”. For a few selected exercise items or the exit ticket, use this simple diagnostic grid:

| Student | Understood task? | Vocabulary/grammar answer correct? | Likely next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| __________ | Yes / No | Yes / No | __________ |
| __________ | Yes / No | Yes / No | __________ |
| __________ | Yes / No | Yes / No | __________ |

- **Task incorrect, word correct when prompted orally:** reteach **T/A/S**, directions, and conditions.
- **Task correct, word incorrect:** provide targeted vocabulary practice.
- **Both incorrect:** scaffold the instruction **and** give a limited word bank.
- **Grammar transfer item missed:** check whether the student overlooked *past simple* **before** deciding they need grammar reteaching.

**Target for this lesson:** Students correctly identify the action, answer form/direction, and any special condition on **at least 3 of 4 checked prompts**; they then answer at least **4 target vocabulary items** correctly with reasonable support.

**Wrong-answer clinic:** 

- *Write the English word for huisarts*: *huisarts* is in the **wrong language**; answer **GP**.
- *Complete with the past simple of go: Yesterday I ___ to the GP*: *was going* does not match **past simple**; answer **went**. Students must notice the tense condition, even if they know both verb forms.

**Exit-ticket key:**

- Prompt A: **Translate** *afspraak* **into English**. Write **one word**. Expected marking: **translate** (T), **into English** (A), **one word** (S); answer **appointment**.
- Prompt B: **Choose the word that does NOT describe a medical professional:** *dentist / GP / prescription*. Expected marking: **choose** (T), **one option** (A), **NOT** (S); answer **prescription**.
