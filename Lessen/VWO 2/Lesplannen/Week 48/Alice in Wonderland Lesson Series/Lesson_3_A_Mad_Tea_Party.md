# Lesson blueprint: Alice in Wonderland 3 | A Mad Tea-Party

**Subject:** English  
**Class:** 2 VWO  
**Duration:** **30 minutes**  
**Core text:** Chapter 7 original quotation + teacher adaptation  
**Theme:** Feelings, healthy boundaries, advice and reassurance  
**Focus:** Intensive reading; linking words; false friends; present perfect and modals; guided final writing

## Lesson preparation

| Element | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Question of the day** | What can Alice do when other people make her uncomfortable? |
| **Objective** | Students interpret a short fictional scene and begin a supportive message to Alice using the required grammar and health vocabulary. |
| **Success criteria** | I can identify the main idea and one contrast word, explain *actual*, correct modal/present perfect errors, and begin a letter following every TASK condition. |
| **Stepping Stones link** | Stones 4–6; Grammar 3 (present perfect), Grammar 4 (modals); G Reading (false friends); Yellow Pages linking words and intensive reading, pp. 196–197. |
| **Materials** | Student reader Lesson 3; coloured pens; TASK checklist; timer; optional dictionary. |
| **Working method** | Silent individual reading and writing; short assigned-pair evidence check. |

## 30-minute EDI lesson sequence

| Time | EDI phase | Teacher actions | Student actions / workbook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3 min** | Retrieval | Show *actual*, *eventually*, *She should goes*. Ask pupils to respond individually. | Write meanings and correction. |
| **3–6 min** | Engage | Show **“No room! No room!”**; ask how Alice might feel and what she could do. | Predict with one health or feelings word. |
| **6–11 min** | Read / model | Read adaptation; demonstrate **intensive reading** of one sentence with *but* or *however*. | Complete **A1** and **A3**. |
| **11–15 min** | Guided reading | Apply TASK to **A2** (*TWO expressions*) and **B4** (*translate into Dutch*). Request evidence. | Complete **A2**, **B4**; compare with assigned partner. |
| **15–19 min** | Grammar clinic | Model one correction and recap **modal + base verb**, **has/have + participle**. | Complete **C6–C7** as a quick individual check. |
| **19–22 min** | Writing instruction / TASK | Display final letter constraints; circle **70–90 words**, **2 present perfect**, **3 modals**, **4 health words**, **reassurance**, **because/however**. | Plan key phrases using the writing frame. |
| **22–28 min** | Independent writing | Set a six-minute silent drafting sprint. Circulate for quick feedback on task conditions. | **Begin** the final letter (aim for 40–60 words in class); complete to 70–90 as homework. |
| **28–30 min** | Check / close | Ask for one self-check and one grammar correction. Collect drafts or check progress. | Underline one present perfect and one modal; write one improvement target. |

**Essential in class:** A1–A3 (emphasise A1/A3), B4, C6–C7 and a **first draft** of the final letter.  
**Homework:** Complete the **70–90-word Wonderland Health Advice Letter**, including all six conditions, and finish **B5** (text evidence). Submit for marking next lesson.  
**Optional extension:** Explain why *however* creates a contrast and add a second reason using *because*; compare the original quote with the adaptation.

## Teacher language and examples

- “*However* introduces a contrast. Read the information before and after it.”
- “*Actual* means *real/werkelijk*, not the Dutch *actueel*.”
- “*She has never attended* is present perfect; *she should rest* uses a base verb.”
- “Today we **start** a strong letter. We **finish and self-check** it for homework; don't rush a full paragraph.”

## Final letter marking rubric (when homework is submitted)

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Follows the task and 70–90-word limit | 0–2 |
| Two accurate present perfect constructions | 0–2 |
| Three appropriate modal uses | 0–2 |
| Four health words, reassurance and linking word | 0–2 |

## Differentiation

**Support:** use workbook sentence frame; give *dizzy, tired, headache, worried, rest, recover* and *can, could, should, have to*.  
**Extension:** use both *because* and *however* and an extra example of reassurance.

## Formative assessment and reflection

Check lesson evidence and grammar in class; assess the **completed** written paragraph later. Diagnose missed instructions separately from incorrect forms.
