# LESSON 1 | Down the Rabbit-Hole

**30-minute route:** Complete **A1–A2, B3–B4, C5, D7–D8 and one D9 advice sentence** in class. Complete **C6 and the second D9 sentence at home**.

**Original fragment, Chapter 1:** “Oh dear! Oh dear! I shall be late!”

**Teacher adaptation for 2 VWO:**

> Alice was resting beside her sister when a White Rabbit hurried past. He looked worried and took a watch from his pocket. Alice followed him and fell down a deep hole. After a long fall, she found herself in a hall with many locked doors. She was dizzy, confused and a little frightened. On a small table she noticed a bottle marked DRINK ME. She was curious, but she remembered that a sensible person should check something before drinking it. She had never seen a place like this. “Maybe I can find help,” she thought. She did not have to drink anything immediately. First, she could look for a key or a sign. Eventually, she discovered a tiny golden key beside the bottle. It opened a very small door. Alice felt relieved, but another problem remained: she was too tall to fit through the door.

# LESSON 2 | Advice from a Caterpillar

**30-minute route:** Complete **A1–A2, B3–B4, C6 and a short D role-play** in class. Finish **B5 and C7 at home** (or as a fast-finisher task).

**Original fragment, Chapter 5:** “Who are you?”

**Teacher adaptation for 2 VWO:**

> After several strange adventures, Alice met a Caterpillar sitting on a mushroom. He asked a simple question: “Who are you?” Alice had trouble answering. “I have changed size so many times,” she said. “I have felt dizzy and confused all day.” The Caterpillar listened, although he did not look particularly sympathetic. Alice wanted practical advice. He told her that different sides of the mushroom would change her height in different ways. That sounded useful, but she could not be certain which part to choose. “You should be careful,” Alice told herself. “I can try a small bite, but I have to think first.” Eventually, she grew to a more comfortable height. The change did not solve every problem, but she felt calmer and more confident.

# LESSON 3 | A Mad Tea-Party

**30-minute route:** Complete **A1–A3, B4, C6–C7**, then **start** the final writing task. Aim for **40–60 words in class** and **finish to 70–90 words at home**. Complete **B5** at home if not finished.

**Original fragment, Chapter 7:** “No room! No room!”

**Teacher adaptation for 2 VWO:**

> By the time Alice reached a garden, she had met many unusual people. At a tea party, the Hatter and the March Hare argued and refused to make room for her. Alice tried to stay polite, but their strange questions made her feel frustrated. “I have never attended a tea party like this,” she thought. “I have already asked three sensible questions, but nobody has answered.” Alice had a headache and felt very tired. The actual problem was not a lack of chairs; it was a lack of kindness. Alice decided she could leave. She did not have to stay where she felt uncomfortable. Before she went, she offered the sleeping Dormouse some advice: “You should rest somewhere quiet.” The guests continued to argue, however, and Alice walked away. She had learnt something useful: even in Wonderland, she could speak calmly and set a boundary.

### D. Final writing: Wonderland Health Advice Letter

**TASK: Write ONE paragraph of 70–90 words to Alice, giving her supportive advice. Start in class and finish for homework.**  
You **must** include:

- at least **two present perfect forms** (*have/has + past participle*);
- at least **three modals** from *can, could, should, have to* (you may repeat a modal, but use at least two different ones);
- at least **four health-related words** (for example: dizzy, worried, tired, headache, rest, recover);
- **one reassuring expression** (*Don't worry* / *You'll be fine*);
- a reason or contrast with **because** or **however**.

**Writing frame (optional):**

> You have __________ and you have __________. You seem __________. I think you should __________ because __________. You can __________. You could __________. You don't have to __________. Don't worry, __________.
