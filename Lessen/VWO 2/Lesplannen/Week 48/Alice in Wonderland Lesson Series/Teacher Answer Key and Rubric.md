# Teacher Answer Key | Alice in Wonderland 

All reading answers below refer to the original Carroll extracts in the student reader. Additional grammar and false-friend tasks are teacher-written and are **not** quotations from the novel. For open responses, accept accurate equivalent phrasing. Do not require students to understand every nineteenth-century word.

## Lesson 1 | Chapter I

1. Predictions vary; acknowledge a reasoned response.
2. **A.** Alice thinks about safety warnings and then decides to taste the bottle.
3. Any two **distinct textual dangers**, e.g. **"a red-hot poker will burn you"**, **"if you cut your finger very deeply with a knife, it usually bleeds"**, **"children who had got burnt"**, **"eaten up by wild beasts"**, or **"bottle marked ‘poison’"**. Insist on two, not more.
4. She checks whether it is marked poison because she remembers warnings from stories and rules taught by her friends.
5. **dared to try**; the context shows she tastes it after thinking about possible danger.
6. (a) **eigenlijk**; (b) **uiteindelijk**. Neither comes from the original excerpt.
7. **has read**. The source has **had read** (past perfect), not present perfect. The rewritten **grammar exercise** is deliberately outside the excerpt.
8. Accept grammatical and safe advice, e.g. *Alice should ask someone for help.* *She could leave the bottle alone.* After *should/could*, no *to*.
9. **NO**; **"she very soon finished it off"** (or **"Alice ventured to taste it"**).

## Lesson 2 | Chapter V

1. Predictions vary.
2. **B.** She cannot easily explain who she is because she has changed several times.
3. Examples of exact phrases: **"I--I hardly know, sir, just at present"**; **"because I'm not myself, you see"**; or **"being so many different sizes in a day is very confusing"**. Allow a short exact quotation that proves uncertainty.
4. **No**, *copy* means reproduce original English words, not translate. Five to ten words, e.g. *Copy means use the exact original English words.*
5. (a) **strictly**, (b) **puzzling**, (c) **right now**.
6. (a) **uiteindelijk**, (b) **werkelijke**. Teacher-written examples.
7. (a) **has changed**; (b) **has asked**. The source's *must have been changed* is a modal perfect passive and should not be misidentified as the simple present perfect pattern taught here.
8. Examples: *Alice should take a moment to think.* *She doesn't have to answer at once.*
9. *confused* or *shy* with supporting clue (*very confusing*, *rather shyly*).

## Lesson 3 | Chapter VII

1. Answers vary.
2. **A.** They quarrel about politeness.
3. Exact source sentence: **"‘You should learn not to make personal remarks,’ Alice said with some severity; ‘it's very rude.’"** If asked to copy just Alice's spoken advice, the first quoted sentence is sufficient; the reader prints a continuous narrative sentence.
4. Two of: the March Hare offers wine when there is none, the March Hare accuses Alice of coming without an invitation, the Hatter comments negatively about Alice's hair.
5. **except**. The first *but* is contrast; the second *but* means *except*.
6. **polite**, **werkelijke** (the latter comes from an added example).
7. **should**; (a) **has met**; (b) **has offered**.
8. Accept accurate original student sentences, e.g. *Alice has felt uncomfortable at the party. She should speak calmly and find a friend.*
9. It prevents copying **more than one sentence** or giving several sentences instead of one.

## Final writing assessment (8 points)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task / length | Many items missing | Partly complete | 70–90 words and covers advice clearly |
| Present perfect | 0 accurate | 1 accurate | At least 2 accurate |
| Modals | None accurate | 1 accurate / little variation | At least 2 accurate, different modals |
| Health language and cohesion | Weak vocabulary/tone | Some vocabulary/structure | 3 appropriate health words, a linking word and supportive tone |

**Model final reply (teacher-created, not part of the book):**

Dear Alice, you have had a strange day, and you have met some very rude people. You should find a calmer place because you seem upset. You could speak to someone who is kind and explain how you feel. You do not have to stay at a party that makes you uncomfortable. If you have a headache, you should rest for a little while. You can take care of yourself and ask for help. I hope you feel better soon.
