### Notes

- **Preparation:** Make the reader, workbook and separate vocabulary/grammar instruction cards available before the lesson. Display the learning objective and success criteria on the board.
- **Differentiation:** Students who need support can use the instruction cards, Dutch vocabulary glosses and sentence starters. Faster students can attempt the expanded Language items, including error correction, tense justification and extended advice.
- **Time management:** The 30-minute sequence prioritises the original core Q1–Q9 tasks. The expanded exercises are extension work and can be completed after the lesson; they are not all expected within the nine-minute Language allocation.
- **Assessment for learning:** Use retrieval answers, the Q2 comprehension response, textual evidence from Q3–Q5, grammar responses and the Q9 exit ticket to check progress towards the success criteria.
- **Google Classroom:** Students upload their completed Lesson 2 workbook and select **Turn in**. I Have to remind them to review unfamiliar vocabulary and complete the eight-line dialogue for homework.
- **30-minute lessons:** Due to the limited duration of each lesson, some exercises may need to be completed as homework. The main aim of this three-lesson sequence is to encourage students to develop their language skills more implicitly through the meaningful context of a literary text. Throughout the sequence, students engage with grammar, reading comprehension, dialogue and vocabulary. My intention was to create a holistic learning experience in which these skills are not taught in isolation but are integrated through the exploration of _Alice in Wonderland_. I aim to make language learning more meaningful, contextualised and engaging. Furthermore, I have sought to align the lesson sequence with the new attainment targets (_eindtermen_), particularly by integrating different language skills and encouraging students to apply their linguistic knowledge in meaningful contexts.
