# Lesson blueprint: Alice in Wonderland 1 | Down the Rabbit-Hole

**Subject:** English  
**Class:** 2 VWO  
**Duration:** **30 minutes**  
**Core text:** Chapter 1 original quotation + teacher adaptation in the student reader  
**Theme:** Health / staying safe / feelings  
**Focus:** Exploring, skimming and scanning; vocabulary in context; false friends; brief present perfect and modal practice

## Lesson preparation

| Element | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Question of the day** | What should Alice do when she feels worried and dizzy? |
| **Objective** | Students skim a short fictional passage for the main idea, scan for evidence and use health language to give Alice safe advice. |
| **Success criteria** | I can choose the main idea; copy two objects and explain one piece of evidence; explain *sensible* and *eventually*; use one present perfect form and at least one correct modal. |
| **Stepping Stones link** | Theme 2 Health; Stones 4 and 6; Grammar 3 and 4; Yellow Pages pp. 196–197 (reading strategies and words in context). |
| **Materials** | Student reader Lesson 1; board; one highlighter; timer or mini-whiteboards. |
| **Working method** | Individual silent start, then assigned pairs (Reader / Evidence Finder); voice level 0 for reading and 1 for pair checks. |
| **Before class** | Display: **“You find a bottle marked DRINK ME. What should you do?”** |

## 30-minute EDI lesson sequence

| Time | EDI phase | Teacher actions | Student actions / workbook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3 min** | Starter / retrieval | Ask for advice about the mystery bottle. Elicit **should + base verb**. | Write one *should* or *shouldn't* sentence; one pupil shares. |
| **3–6 min** | Activate | Show the original White Rabbit quotation, explain *shall* in this context and ask for a prediction. | Predict how Alice may feel. |
| **6–11 min** | Explain and model | Read the adaptation aloud. Model **skimming**: find the whole scene's main idea, then **scanning**: find a precise word. | Complete workbook **A1–A2** individually. |
| **11–15 min** | TASK modelling | Display workbook **B3**. Underline **exactly TWO** and **copy**; show where to find one object in the text. | Decode TASK and find the second object; note both. |
| **15–20 min** | Guided paired practice | Give Reader / Evidence Finder roles; coach pupils to verify answers in the passage. | Complete **B3–B4**; point to the evidence. |
| **20–24 min** | Vocabulary instruction | Briefly model using context for *sensible* and *eventually*; explain why Dutch look-alikes mislead. | Complete **C5**; say one context clue to partner. |
| **24–28 min** | Independent application | Check present perfect form on board; ask pupils to apply one modal. | Complete **D7–D8** and write **one** sentence from **D9**. |
| **28–30 min** | Exit ticket | Ask: What was the main idea? What does *eventually* mean? Which advice sentence is correct? | Hand in three brief answers. |

**Essential in class:** A1–A2, B3–B4, C5, D7–D8 and one D9 sentence.  
**Homework / optional:** C6 (justify a word from context), complete D9 with a second different modal, and three health-feelings sentences, including one present perfect sentence.

## Teacher language and key examples

- “**Skimming** finds the main idea; **scanning** finds a specific detail.”
- “**Exactly two objects** means two, not three. **Copy** means use the text's words.”
- “**Sensible** means *verstandig*, not *sensibel*. **Eventually** means *uiteindelijk*, not *eventueel*.”
- “Alice **has never seen** this place. She **should check** the label.”

## Differentiation

**Support:** highlight the relevant paragraph; use *She feels … because …* and *She should …* frames; provide *bottle, key, door, dizzy*.  
**Extension:** complete C6 and write a second advice sentence using **could** plus a reason.

## Formative assessment and reflection

Record separately: misunderstood instruction, missed evidence, false-friend misconception, or grammar error. Keep the passage fictional: never imply drinking unknown liquids is safe in real life.
