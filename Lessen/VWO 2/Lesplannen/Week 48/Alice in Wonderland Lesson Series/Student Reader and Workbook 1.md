# Alice's Adventures in Wonderland: Original Text Reader and Workbook

**Class:** 2 VWO  
**Series:** 3 lessons of 30 minutes each  
**Author:** Lewis Carroll, *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865)

**Important:** The three reading passages below reproduce **Carroll's original, unadapted words**. They are **short, continuous extracts** from Chapters I, V and VII, not retellings or rewritten versions. Only the digital edition's unusual opening quotation marks have been displayed as ordinary print quotation marks. The activities, context notes, vocabulary help and grammar examples are written by the teacher and are always outside the original text.

**Original source:** Project Gutenberg Millennium Fulcrum Edition 3.0, Chapter I: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rgs/alice-I.html ; Chapter V: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rgs/alice-V.html ; Chapter VII: https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rgs/alice-VII.html . The complete public-domain novel is also available at https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11 .

## TASK: Read every instruction first

**T** Task word: underline *choose, copy, explain, complete, write*.  
**A** Answer format: letter, word, sentence, or short paragraph?  
**S** Special conditions: *exactly two*, *from the text*, *in English*, *present perfect*.  
**K** Keep checking: does your answer match the instruction?

Do not translate every unfamiliar word. First explore the text, then skim for its main idea, scan for evidence, and read a short passage intensively when needed.

# Lesson 1: Down the Rabbit-Hole | The bottle

**Context note, written for students:** Alice has followed a strange White Rabbit into Wonderland. She finds a bottle with the instruction DRINK ME. The excerpt begins as she decides what to do. This note is not part of the novel.

**Original excerpt:** Chapter I, from "It was all very well to say" to "she very soon finished it off."

> It was all very well to say ‘Drink me,’ but the wise little Alice was not going to do that in a hurry.

> ‘No, I'll look first,’ she said, ‘and see whether it's marked “poison” or not’; for she had read several nice little histories about children who had got burnt, and eaten up by wild beasts and other unpleasant things, all because they would not remember the simple rules their friends had taught them: such as, that a red-hot poker will burn you if you hold it too long; and that if you cut your finger very deeply with a knife, it usually bleeds; and she had never forgotten that, if you drink much from a bottle marked ‘poison,’ it is almost certain to disagree with you, sooner or later.

> However, this bottle was not marked ‘poison,’ so Alice ventured to taste it, and finding it very nice, (it had, in fact, a sort of mixed flavour of cherry-tart, custard, pine-apple, roast turkey, toffee, and hot buttered toast,) she very soon finished it off.

# Lesson 3: A Mad Tea-Party | Good manners

**Context note, written for students:** Alice has joined the March Hare and the Hatter at a tea party. Their conversation is not very friendly. This note is not part of the novel.

**Original excerpt:** Chapter VII, from "Have some wine" to "it's very rude."

> ‘Have some wine,’ the March Hare said in an encouraging tone.

> Alice looked all round the table, but there was nothing on it but tea. ‘I don't see any wine,’ she remarked.

> ‘There isn't any,’ said the March Hare.

> ‘Then it wasn't very civil of you to offer it,’ said Alice angrily.

> ‘It wasn't very civil of you to sit down without being invited,’ said the March Hare.

> ‘I didn't know it was your table,’ said Alice; ‘it's laid for a great many more than three.’

> ‘Your hair wants cutting,’ said the Hatter. He had been looking at Alice for some time with great curiosity, and this was his first speech.

> ‘You should learn not to make personal remarks,’ Alice said with some severity; ‘it's very rude.’
