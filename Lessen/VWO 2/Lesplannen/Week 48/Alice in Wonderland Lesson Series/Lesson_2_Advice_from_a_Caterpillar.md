# Lesson blueprint: Alice in Wonderland 2 | Advice from a Caterpillar

**Subject:** English  
**Class:** 2 VWO  
**Duration:** **30 minutes**  
**Core text:** Chapter 5 original quotation + teacher adaptation  
**Theme:** Describing what has happened; feelings; asking for and giving advice  
**Focus:** Scanning and context clues; false friends; present perfect; modal meanings; Stone-style speaking

## Lesson preparation

| Element | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Question of the day** | What advice should Alice follow, and how can she explain what has happened? |
| **Objective** | Students locate facts in a short text, recognise a misleading look-alike word and use present perfect and modals to support Alice. |
| **Success criteria** | I can give the main problem and three details; find a sentence that proves Alice has changed; correct present perfect and modal mistakes; give advice aloud. |
| **Stepping Stones link** | Stones 4–6; Grammar 3 (present perfect); Grammar 4 (*can, could, should, have to*); Yellow Pages context and false friends, pp. 196–197. |
| **Materials** | Student reader Lesson 2; board; timer; mini-whiteboards. |
| **Working method** | Assigned pairs; Question Decoder / Evidence Finder and then Alice / Adviser, with no moving seats. |

## 30-minute EDI lesson sequence

| Time | EDI phase | Teacher actions | Student actions / workbook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3 min** | Retrieval | Display *She should goes* and *I has felt dizzy*. Ask for quick corrections. | Correct both on paper. |
| **3–6 min** | Activate | Read **“Who are you?”** aloud and ask why Alice might find it hard to answer. | Predict the character's feelings. |
| **6–11 min** | Reading / modelling | Read the adaptation; show how to **skim** for the main problem and **scan** for named details. | Complete **A1–A2** (one-sentence problem and three details). |
| **11–16 min** | Guided evidence / TASK | Model workbook **B3**: *Find ONE sentence* and *copy exactly*. Ask pupils to mark evidence. | Pairs complete **B3** and check exact wording. |
| **16–20 min** | Context / false friends | Contrast *sympathetic* with its Dutch look-alike; recap *eventually*. | Complete **B4** and justify the choice orally. |
| **20–24 min** | Grammar mini-lesson | Model **have/has + past participle** and **should + base verb**; contrast advice and obligation. | Complete **C6**; stronger pupils also start **C7**. |
| **24–28 min** | Speaking application | Prompt a short health consultation; set a visible 45-second turn timer and switch roles. | Complete **D** in pairs using one present perfect and two modals across the exchange. |
| **28–30 min** | Exit ticket | Ask pupils to give one correct advice sentence and the meaning of a false friend. | Write one sentence and one translation. |

**Essential in class:** A1–A2, B3–B4, C6 and brief D role-play.  
**Homework / optional:** B5 (why Alice hesitates), C7 (modal-to-function matching), and a fuller written consultation using a second present perfect sentence.

## Teacher language and examples

- “The instruction says **copy exactly**, so I cannot change the sentence.”
- “*Sympathetic* means *meelevend/begripvol* in this context. Use the surrounding sentence to check.”
- “*I have felt dizzy all day* uses **have + past participle**. *She should rest* does **not** take *to*.”
- “**Can** can express ability, **could** a possibility, **should** advice and **have to** an obligation.”

## Differentiation

**Support:** colour-code *have/has* and participle; provide *be–been, feel–felt, have–had, go–gone*; use the workbook speaking frame.  
**Extension:** distinguish *should* (advice) from *have to* (necessity); complete B5 and C7.

## Safety/context note

The mushroom in Wonderland is fantasy, not real-world health guidance. Never present eating unknown plants or mushrooms as good advice.

## Formative assessment and reflection

Distinguish difficulty understanding the TASK instruction from errors in verb forms or vocabulary. One quick record per pupil is enough for this short lesson.
