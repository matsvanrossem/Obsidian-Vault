## 7. Grammar: Present perfect

> [!note]
> ## Present perfect
> 
> **Form:** Subject + have/has + past participle
> 
> 
> 
> | Form        | Example                |
> | ----------- | ---------------------- |
> | Affirmative | Alice has changed.     |
> | Negative    | Alice has not changed. |
> | Question    | Has Alice changed?     |
> 
> Use the present perfect for experiences without a specific finished time, past actions with a connection to the present, and situations continuing into the present.
> 
> Common signal words include _already, yet, ever, never, since, for_ and _so far_.
> 
> **Instructions:** Complete the sentences using the present perfect. Decide whether you need **has** or **have**, and use the correct past participle. Pay attention to irregular verbs and the connection between past experiences and the present situation.

## 8. Grammar: Giving advice and expressing obligation

**Instructions:** Imagine that Alice has asked you for advice because she no longer feels certain about her identity. Use modal verbs to give thoughtful and supportive advice. Explain the reasons behind your suggestions.

> [!note]
> ## Modal verbs
> 
> Modal verbs allow us to express different degrees of advice, possibility, obligation and prohibition.
> 
> |Structure|Function|Example|
> |---|---|---|
> |Should|Advice|You should trust yourself.|
> |Shouldn't|Negative advice|You shouldn't compare yourself to others.|
> |Could|Suggestion|You could ask for help.|
> |Must|Strong obligation|You must follow the rules.|
> |Mustn't|Prohibition|You mustn't enter this room.|
> |Don't have to|Absence of obligation|You don't have to explain yourself.|
> 
> **Remember:** After a modal verb, use the base form of the main verb.
> 
> _She should talk._ (Correct)
> 
> _She should to talk._ (Incorrect)
### A. Complete the sentences
