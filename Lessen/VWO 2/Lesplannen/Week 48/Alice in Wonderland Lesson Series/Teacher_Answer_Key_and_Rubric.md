# Alice in Wonderland | Teacher answer key and assessment notes (30-minute series)

**Class:** 2 VWO. **Schedule:** 3 × 30 minutes. Check the in-class core first; the remaining items are optional or homework. The 70–90-word letter is **started** in Lesson 3 and **finished for homework**. Adapted passages in the student workbook are the source for these answers. Accept equivalent accurate English explanations where indicated.

## Lesson 1

**A1:** B.  **A2:** *dizzy*, *confused* or *frightened* (depending on which time students describe; also *worried* refers to the Rabbit, not Alice).  
**B3:** Accept any two distinct items Alice notices in the hall, for example *bottle*, *key*, *door* or *sign* (a sign is a possible object to look for, but she does not actually notice one; therefore **do not** accept *sign* if the instruction is strictly “notices”). Best: *bottle* and *tiny golden key*.  
**B4:** *Because she is too tall to fit through the small door.*  
**C5:** *sensible* = **verstandig**; *eventually* = **uiteindelijk**.  
**C6:** Example: “She checks the drink first, which is the careful thing to do”; “Eventually refers to what happens after she searches.”  
**D7:** *has never seen*.  
**D8:** A; after *should*, use the base form **read**, not *to read*.  
**D9:** Accept safe, grammatical suggestions: *She should ask for help. She can look for the key.* Avoid implying that unknown drinks are safe in real life.

## Lesson 2

**A1:** Example: Alice has changed size and feels confused about herself and what to do.  
**A2:** **Caterpillar**; **on a mushroom**; *dizzy / confused / calmer / more confident* (identify when).  
**B3:** *“I have changed size so many times,” she said.* Exact wording required as instructed.  
**B4:** *sympathetic* = **meelevend / begripvol**, not merely likeable; *eventually* = **uiteindelijk**.  
**B5:** Example: *She hesitates because she does not know which part of the mushroom to choose.*  
**C6:** a. *I have felt dizzy all day.* b. *She should be careful.*  
**C7:** can **C**, could **D**, should **A**, have to **B**. Note: these are the most relevant intended functions **in this exercise**, but *can / could* can have other meanings in different contexts.  
**D speaking:** Check that each partner uses >=1 present perfect sentence, >=2 modals, reports a health problem, and reassures politely.

## Lesson 3

**A1:** B.  
**A2:** *had a headache* and *felt very tired*; accept *frustrated* or *uncomfortable* if learners justify.  
**A3:** **Contrast**.  
**B4:** **echte / werkelijke**.  
**B5:** Example: *She leaves because the guests are unkind and their questions make her uncomfortable.*  
**C6:** a. *Alice can leave.* b. *She should rest.* c. *She has never attended this party.*  
**C7:** *has already asked*; *does not have to* / *doesn't have to* (NOT *mustn't*, which changes the meaning). 

### Final letter marking rubric, 8 points (score when complete)

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task and organisation | Missing several instructions | Partly complete / some clarity | Coherent 70–90-word paragraph, all central instructions addressed |
| Present perfect | No valid target forms | One accurate form | Two accurate forms in context |
| Modals | No accurate uses | 1–2 accurate uses | At least three accurate uses, with at least two different modals |
| Health vocabulary and tone | Little relevant language | Some relevant vocabulary / limited reassurance | Four appropriate health words + clear reassuring tone and linking word |

**Model response, approximately 82 words (teacher-written):**  
*Dear Alice, you have experienced a very strange day, and you have felt worried and tired. You should take a break because the tea party was stressful. You can find a quiet place and rest until your headache improves. You could ask a friendly character for help, and you do not have to stay with anyone who makes you uncomfortable. Don't worry: you have already solved several difficult problems, and you can handle this one too. I hope you feel better soon.*

## Typical errors to diagnose

| Pattern | Likely issue | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Correct topic but wrong number of answers | Misread task | Repeat TASK; circle *exactly TWO*. |
| Right section but unsupported claim | Evidence selection | Require text quotation or line reference. |
| *should to go*, *she can goes* | Modal form | Re-teach modal + base verb. |
| *I has felt* | Agreement | Sort *I/you/we/they have* vs *he/she/it has*. |
| *eventually = eventueel* | False friend | Ask for sentence context and dictionary sense. |
| *actual = actueel* | False friend | Contrast *actual (real)* with *current (actueel)*. |
| Confident answer but wrong tense | Content-language disconnect | Have student verbalise requested tense before answering. |

## Tips for classroom management

- Starter is individual and silent.
- Use assigned pairs; keep a visible timer and explicit voice level.
- Question Decoder and Evidence Finder roles switch; both must be accountable.
- Correct **instruction errors** separately from **vocabulary/grammar errors**.
- Treat all unfamiliar food in Wonderland as fantasy, not a real-life health recommendation.
