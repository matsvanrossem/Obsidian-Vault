## Lesson preparation

| Element | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Lesson topic** | F Listening strategies and comprehension |
| **Stepping Stones coverage** | F Listening **35, 36a–36c, 37–39**. |
| **Main focus** | Listen with a clear purpose, capture relevant details, and check answers against heard evidence. |
| **Prior learning** | Chapter 2 Crime vocabulary and earlier watching/reading strategies. |
| **Materials** | Stepping Stones F Listening audio, playback equipment, exercise book, evidence-note slip. |
| **Teacher preparation** | Test the audio, review task requirements and select replay points after checking the recordings. |

### Learning objectives

1. Use prompts to predict useful information before listening.
2. Distinguish between general meaning and details in a spoken text.

### Success criteria (student-friendly)

- [ ] I can identify what information each question asks for.
- [ ] I can listen for meaningful keywords without writing every word.
- [ ] I can explain why an answer fits what I heard.

### Starter on the board

> Show an invented listening question asking for a reason, and a second asking for a number; students choose what to listen for.

## Lesson sequence (45 minutes)

| Time | Phase / working method | Teacher actions | Student actions | Book link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | **Start** | Display the listening goal and audio setup. | Open F Listening. | F Listening |
| 4–9 min | **Warm-up: prediction** | Quickly compare two question types and model underlining keywords before the recording. | Write the information they expect to need. | 35 |
| 9–14 min | **Strategy modelling** | Explain first listen for gist, next listen for details; think aloud with one actual prompt. | Read and mark the task. | 35, 36a–36c |
| 14–23 min | **First listening** | Play the appropriate recording. Gather initial responses to 35 and 36a–36c. | Listen for main points and note evidence words. | 35, 36a–36c |
| 23–30 min | **Second listening and paired check** | Replay selected parts. Ask pairs to identify which answer needs verification. | Compare, ask for replay, and correct. | 36a–36c |
| 30–39 min | **Independent practice** | Set 37–39 according to the audio tasks in the book. | Complete listening tasks and mark uncertain answers. | 37–39 |
| 39–43 min | **Review evidence** | Discuss two disputed answers and show how to reject distractors based on what was actually heard. | Explain what evidence changed their mind. | F Listening |
| 43–45 min | **Exit ticket** | Ask each student to name a useful listening tactic and one difficulty. | Write brief reflection. | F Listening |

## Differentiation

**Additional support:** Provide a focus question before replay and use small, manageable listening segments.

**Challenge / extension:** Ask students to identify an additional relevant fact from the recording and explain why it matters.

## Assessment and follow-up

- **Evidence of learning:** Use paired justification and independent 37–39 responses to identify detail-listening gaps.
- **Homework / preparation:** Finish F exercises as necessary and review listening strategies.
- **Teacher follow-up:** Note which students need vocabulary, grammar, question-reading or skills support, and plan a short retrieval task for the next lesson.
- **Teacher reflection:** What worked? ____________________ What needs adjustment? ____________________
