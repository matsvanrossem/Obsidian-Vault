### Learning objectives

1. Activate relevant Crime vocabulary and background knowledge before viewing.
2. Use the question and any available visual context to make a reasonable prediction before watching.

### Success criteria (student-friendly)

- [ ] I can share three relevant words or ideas about the theme Crime.
- [ ] I can identify what a watching question asks me to look or listen for.
- [ ] I can check a prediction against what I actually see or hear.

### Starter on the board

> Write CRIME on the board. Ask for neutral words linked to people, places and events; sort words into those three categories.

## Lesson sequence (45 minutes)

| Time | Phase / working method | Teacher actions | Student actions | Book link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | **Arrival and goal** | Show the chapter theme and learning goals. Explain that today students start the new chapter and learn how to prepare for watching. | Open Chapter 2; write the learning goal. | Getting Started |
| 4–9 min | **Activate knowledge: crime word cloud** | Prompt quick associations, grouping neutral crime vocabulary into people, events and locations. | Think alone for one minute, then share with partner and class. | Getting Started 1–4 |
| 9–15 min | **Guided book start** | Guide the first Getting Started items. Model the TASK habit: circle the command, check answer form, notice extra conditions, check the result. | Read instructions before answering. Explain what each task requires. | Getting Started 1–4 |
| 15–23 min | **Pairs: complete and compare** | Set remaining Getting Started items; ask partners to justify two answers using clues. | Complete 1–4 and compare with evidence or reasoning. | Getting Started 1–4 |
| 23–28 min | **Before viewing: prediction** | Display A Watching prompts without claiming their exact wording. Invite students to predict what information the clip may provide. | Identify keywords in the actual questions; make a prediction. | A Watching 5a–5b |
| 28–37 min | **Watch with purpose** | Play the relevant excerpt once for overall understanding, then again if needed for the specified tasks. | Attempt 5a–5b and 6a–6b; record clues instead of guessing blindly. | A Watching 5a–5b, 6a–6b |
| 37–43 min | **Peer check and feedback** | Have pairs compare one uncertain answer and point to a visual or spoken clue. Correct misunderstandings. | Explain the clue and adjust answers. | A Watching |
| 43–45 min | **Exit ticket** | Ask: Name one new word and one strategy used before watching. | Write a two-part exit ticket. | Chapter 2 |

## Differentiation

**Additional support:** Provide a word bank and allow students to identify the target information in the question before watching again.

**Challenge / extension:** Ask students to distinguish facts seen/heard from predictions and write one extra question answerable from the clip.

## Assessment and follow-up

- **Evidence of learning:** Collect one prediction with supporting evidence and the two-part exit ticket.
- **Homework / preparation:** Finish any unfinished Getting Started exercises. Prepare for the remaining A Watching tasks.
- **Teacher follow-up:** Note which students need vocabulary, grammar, question-reading or skills support, and plan a short retrieval task for the next lesson.
- **Teacher reflection:** What worked? ____________________ What needs adjustment? ____________________
