### Learning objectives

1. Recognise and use expressions from Stone 4 for caution and warning.
2. Speak intelligibly, using emphasis and pronunciation appropriate to the message.

### Success criteria (student-friendly)

- [ ] I can give a clear warning in an everyday situation.
- [ ] I can respond appropriately to a partner’s warning.
- [ ] I can explain which words or intonation make the warning clear.

### Starter on the board

> Show a fictional situation: a wet floor beside a doorway. Ask learners for two different English ways to warn someone.

## Lesson sequence (45 minutes)

| Time | Phase / working method | Teacher actions | Student actions | Book link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | **Lesson start** | Present speaking goal and the two types of language: caution and warning. | Open Stone 4. | H Speaking |
| 4–9 min | **Retrieval and vocabulary** | Recall familiar Crime words and elicit warning language. | Offer examples and compare meaning. | H Theme Words |
| 9–16 min | **Teacher modelling** | Use verified Stone 4 sentences; model pronunciation and emphasis, then check understanding. | Repeat and mark stressed words. | 45a–45b, 46a–46b |

| 24–34 min | **Interactive roleplay carousel** | Pairs act out fictional everyday situations requiring caution. Rotate partners after short conversations. | Give and respond to warnings using Stone 4 language. | Stone 4 / H Theme Words |
| 34–40 min | **Independent transfer** | Set 49a–49d and 50a–50b according to their actual formats. | Complete remaining priority items and practise pronunciation. | 49a–49d, 50a–50b |
| 40–43 min | **Peer feedback** | Ask peers to rate clarity, target phrase and appropriate tone. | Give one specific improvement suggestion. | H Speaking |
| 43–45 min | **Exit ticket** | Ask students to write and say a brief warning for a new fictional situation. | Produce a relevant phrase. | Stone 4 |

## Differentiation

**Additional support:** Supply sentence frames from the book and allow students to rehearse with the same partner before rotating.

**Challenge / extension:** Use less predictable situations and require a justification or reassurance after the warning.

## Assessment and follow-up

- **Evidence of learning:** Listen to short roleplays, noting intelligibility, correct meaning and appropriate response.
- **Homework / preparation:** Study Stone 4 and the H Theme Words; practise any assigned pronunciation work.
- **Teacher follow-up:** Note which students need vocabulary, grammar, question-reading or skills support, and plan a short retrieval task for the next lesson.
- **Teacher reflection:** What worked? ____________________ What needs adjustment? ____________________
