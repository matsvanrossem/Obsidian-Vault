### Learning objectives

1. Use useful Stone 3 expressions while describing an event.
2. Ask and answer follow-up questions so a listener can understand what happened.

### Success criteria (student-friendly)

- [ ] I can describe the event in a logical order.
- [ ] I can use at least three relevant expressions from Stone 3 or the theme-word list.
- [ ] I can ask a clarification question and answer it understandably.

### Starter on the board

> Give a safe invented incident, such as a missing bicycle discovered after school; ask: What facts would a witness need to explain?

## Lesson sequence (45 minutes)

| Time | Phase / working method | Teacher actions | Student actions | Book link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4 min | **Launch** | Show goal: communicate what happened accurately and clearly. | Identify lesson target. | Stone 3 |
| 4–9 min | **Retrieval: vocabulary** | Quickly rehearse 5 words from B and the D list. | Say or write a suitable English word for each prompt. | D Theme Words |
| 9–15 min | **Model phrases** | Read and model 2–3 genuine Stone 3 structures, checking stress and word choice. | Repeat, mark the phrase pattern, and ask about meanings. | 22a–22b |
| 15–23 min | **Guided application** | Set 22a–23b. Model an answer based on the book task, then ask pairs to check their instructions. | Complete assigned items and rehearse useful phrases. | 22a–22b, 23a–23b |
| 23–33 min | **Roleplay: witness interview** | Pairs alternate witness and interviewer. Give fact cards; listeners ask what, when, where and what happened next. | Use Stone 3 expressions to give a coherent fictional account. | 24–25; Stone 3 |
| 33–40 min | **Independent/partner transfer** | Set 26a–26b. Circulate and ask for clearer or fuller descriptions. | Complete book tasks, switching roles where required. | 26a–26b |
| 40–43 min | **Peer feedback** | Have partners give one praise and one specific improvement using success criteria. | Use a three-item peer checklist. | Stone 3 |
| 43–45 min | **Exit performance** | Invite students to say one clear sentence describing an event. | Speak or record it in writing. | D Speaking |

## Differentiation

**Additional support:** Allow a Stone 3 phrase bank and sequence starters: First, Then, After that.

**Challenge / extension:** Require follow-up questions and more precise event descriptions without reading from notes.

## Assessment and follow-up

- **Evidence of learning:** Use a quick listening checklist for vocabulary, clarity, phrase use and response to questions.
- **Homework / preparation:** Study Stone 3 and the D Theme Words; practise explaining a fictional incident aloud.
- **Teacher follow-up:** Note which students need vocabulary, grammar, question-reading or skills support, and plan a short retrieval task for the next lesson.
- **Teacher reflection:** What worked? ____________________ What needs adjustment? ____________________
